Start a business with 0 cedis.

You do not need capital to begin. You need a problem people already pay to solve, a way to reach them, and the discipline to start this week. Seven steps, built for Ghana.

Step 1

Start with what you already have

Your first business is made from your skill, your time and your knowledge, not from money. What do people already ask you to do, fix, cook, style, teach, carry or arrange?

Do today: write down 10 things you can do well. Circle the 3 that people have already asked you for or paid you for.
Step 2

Choose a service, not a product

A service needs no stock, no shop and no loan. Examples: hair and beauty at home, tutoring, cleaning, laundry pickup, photography with your phone, errands and delivery, phone or screen repair, writing CVs, helping small businesses with WhatsApp and posts, event help.

Do today: pick one service. One. You can add more after your first customers.
Step 3

Sell before you build

Do not print, rent or buy anything yet. First prove that someone will pay. Message 10 people you know with one clear sentence: what you do, who it is for, the price, and when you can start.

  • "I help busy workers in Koforidua get their laundry washed and delivered. GHS 40 per load, pickup Saturday. Want me to start with yours?"
Do today: send that message, adapted to your service, to 10 people. Ask each one to share it with someone.
Step 4

Use free tools that look professional

  • WhatsApp Business (free): add your name, what you do, location and hours. Save quick replies for price, location and payment.
  • Google Business Profile (free): so people searching near you can find you and call.
  • Free design apps on your phone for one clean flyer with your name, service, price and number.
  • Facebook, TikTok, WhatsApp Status: show real work, real places and real results, one short video at a time.
Step 5

Price so that you actually profit

Working for free is not a business. Price your time, your transport, your data and any materials. Then add something for yourself.

Simple rule: price = your costs + your time + your profit. Never copy the cheapest person in the market. Tell customers clearly what they get, and state your price once without apologising.
Step 6

Take a deposit and keep your money separate

For anything bigger than a small job, ask for a deposit by MoMo before you start. It protects you from wasted time and tells you the customer is serious.

  • Use a separate MoMo wallet or at least a separate notebook for the business from the first sale.
  • Write down every sale and every cost, every day. Five minutes, no exceptions.
  • Pay yourself a set amount each week and keep a small part inside the business.
Step 7

Ask for the next customer, every time

Finish every job with two questions: "Are you happy with it?" and "Who else do you know who needs this?" Referrals are free marketing from people who trust you more than any advert could.

Do today: after your first sale, ask for a short voice note or message you can use as a testimonial.
Protect your money while you start. If someone says they sent you money by mistake, check your own MoMo history first. If nothing landed, there is nothing to send back. No network, bank or agent will ever ask for your PIN or a code sent to your phone. If a call feels rushed, hang up and call the official number yourself.
